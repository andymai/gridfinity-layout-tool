# How to Plan Your Gridfinity Drawer Layout: A Complete Guide

Planning your first Gridfinity drawer layout doesn't have to be complicated. This guide covers everything from measuring your drawer to finalizing a layout ready for printing.

Whether you're organizing a desk drawer, tool chest, or kitchen utensils, the planning process is the same. Get this step right, and you'll avoid wasted filament and frustrating reprints.

<!-- IMAGE: Hero image showing a well-organized Gridfinity drawer with bins of various sizes, shot from above. Alt: "Organized drawer with colorful Gridfinity bins in various sizes" -->

## What You'll Learn

- How to measure your drawer accurately
- Converting measurements to Gridfinity grid units
- Choosing the right bin sizes for your items
- Layout strategies for different use cases
- Common mistakes and how to avoid them

**Time required**: 15-30 minutes of planning can save hours of printing and reprinting.

## Quick Reference: Gridfinity Basics

If you're new to Gridfinity, here's what you need to know:

| Specification | Value |
|---------------|-------|
| Grid unit size | 42mm × 42mm |
| Height unit | 7mm per "u" |
| Standard bin heights | 2u, 3u, 4u, 5u, 6u |
| Creator | Zack Freedman (2022) |

The system uses two components: **baseplates** (flat grids lining your drawer) and **bins** (containers that snap onto the grid). Everything follows the 42mm standard, so any bin works with any baseplate.

For a deeper introduction, see the [official Gridfinity documentation](https://gridfinity.xyz/).

## Step 1: Measure Your Drawer

Accurate measurements prevent the most common Gridfinity mistake: printing baseplates that don't fit.

### What to Measure

<!-- IMAGE: Diagram showing drawer measurement points - width, depth, and height with arrows. Alt: "Drawer measurement diagram showing internal width, depth, and height" -->

**Internal width**: Side to side at the narrowest point. Account for drawer slides or rails.

**Internal depth**: Front to back. Check for obstructions at the rear.

**Internal height**: Bottom to the lowest point above (usually where the drawer closes). This determines your maximum bin height.

### Measurement Tips

- **Use millimeters**: Easier for calculations (42mm grid vs. 1.65 inches)
- **Measure twice**: A 5mm error means baseplates that don't fit
- **Check corners**: Some drawers have rounded corners that reduce usable space
- **Account for tolerance**: Subtract 2-3mm for easier installation

### Convert to Grid Units

Divide each measurement by 42 to get your grid dimensions:

```
Grid width  = Internal width (mm) ÷ 42
Grid depth  = Internal depth (mm) ÷ 42
```

**Example**: A drawer measuring 380mm × 520mm converts to:
- Width: 380 ÷ 42 = **9 units** (with 2mm left over)
- Depth: 520 ÷ 42 = **12 units** (with 16mm left over)

The leftover space becomes padding around your baseplates—perfectly fine.

### Common Drawer Sizes

| Drawer Type | Typical Size | Grid Size |
|-------------|--------------|-----------|
| Standard desk | 380 × 500mm | 9 × 11 |
| IKEA Alex | 297 × 525mm | 7 × 12 |
| Tool chest | 450 × 600mm | 10 × 14 |
| Kitchen utensil | 350 × 450mm | 8 × 10 |

## Step 2: Inventory Your Items

Don't design bins until you know what goes in them. This step prevents the "wrong size bin" problem.

### Quick Inventory Process

1. **Empty the drawer** (or gather items you plan to store)
2. **Group similar items** (pens together, cables together, etc.)
3. **Identify the largest item** in each group
4. **Note items needing special handling** (tall, fragile, frequently accessed)

### Measure Problem Items

For items that don't obviously fit standard bins, measure their footprint:

<!-- IMAGE: Photo showing someone measuring a cable coil with a ruler. Alt: "Measuring coiled USB cable to determine bin size needed" -->

| Item | Approximate Size | Suggested Bin |
|------|------------------|---------------|
| Pens/pencils | 15mm × 140mm | 1×4 |
| USB cables (coiled) | 60mm × 60mm | 2×2 |
| Scissors | 30mm × 180mm | 1×5 |
| SD cards | 24mm × 32mm | 1×1 |
| Phone charger (brick) | 50mm × 60mm | 2×2 |

### Prioritize by Access Frequency

Items you use daily should be:
- Near the **front** of the drawer
- In **larger bins** (easier to grab)
- **Not stacked** under other items

Items used rarely can go in the back or in tighter configurations.

## Step 3: Choose Bin Heights

Gridfinity bins come in standardized heights. Choosing the right height maximizes storage without wasting drawer space.

### Height Reference

| Height | Millimeters | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| 2u | 14mm | SD cards, coins, flat items |
| 3u | 21mm | Pens, markers, USB drives |
| 4u | 28mm | Tools, cables, most items |
| 5u | 35mm | Bottles, taller containers |
| 6u | 42mm | Deep storage, bulky items |

### Match to Drawer Height

If your drawer is 45mm tall, use bins up to **5u (35mm)** to leave clearance. Don't use the absolute maximum—leave 5-10mm for the bin lip and easy removal.

### Multi-Layer Strategy

For deep drawers (80mm+), consider **stacking layers**:

<!-- IMAGE: Cross-section diagram showing two Gridfinity layers in a deep drawer. Alt: "Cross-section of deep drawer with two Gridfinity layers" -->

- **Bottom layer**: 5-6u bins for large items
- **Top layer**: 2-3u bins on a raised platform

This approach works well for tool chests and workshop drawers. The [Gridfinity Layout Tool](https://gridfinitylayouttool.com) supports multi-layer planning.

## Step 4: Design Your Layout

Now combine your measurements, inventory, and height choices into a functional layout.

### Layout Strategy Options

**Zone-based layout**: Group items by category
- Front zone: Daily use items
- Middle zone: Regular use items
- Back zone: Rarely used items

**Task-based layout**: Group items by activity
- Writing supplies together
- Cable/charging together
- Craft supplies together

**Size-based layout**: Optimize space efficiency
- Large bins in corners
- Small bins fill gaps
- Irregular shapes at edges

### Visual Planning Tools

The fastest way to iterate on layouts is with a visual planner:

**[Gridfinity Layout Tool](https://gridfinitylayouttool.com)**: Drag-and-drop bins, set exact dimensions, preview in 3D, generate print lists

**[Gridfinity Base Planner](https://gridfinity-base-planner.vercel.app/)**: Simple baseplate calculator

**[Gridfinity Baseplate Calculator](https://ethanthompson.org/gridfinity-frame-calculator)**: Calculate how many baseplates fit your space

Alternatively, sketch on graph paper (1 square = 1 grid unit) or use a spreadsheet with merged cells.

## Step 5: Plan for Printing

Before finalizing, verify your layout is printable.

### Check Print Bed Size

| Printer Bed | Max Baseplate | Max Single Bin |
|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| 180 × 180mm | 4×4 units | 4×4 units |
| 220 × 220mm | 5×5 units | 5×5 units |
| 256 × 256mm | 6×6 units | 6×6 units |
| 300 × 300mm | 7×7 units | 7×7 units |

Larger baseplates must be printed in sections and placed side by side. Most layout tools show warnings when bins exceed your print bed.

### Estimate Filament Usage

Rough estimates for planning:

| Component | Approximate Filament |
|-----------|---------------------|
| 1×1×3u bin | 8g |
| 2×2×4u bin | 25g |
| 3×3×4u bin | 45g |
| 5×5 baseplate | 100g |

A typical desk drawer (9×11 grid) uses **300-500g** of filament—about one-third to one-half of a standard spool.

For detailed cost breakdowns, see our [Gridfinity Filament Cost Guide](/blog/gridfinity-filament-cost).

### Print Order Recommendation

1. **Baseplates first** — verify they fit the drawer
2. **One test bin** — check dimensions before batch printing
3. **Batch similar bins** — group by height for efficiency
4. **Specialty bins last** — custom holders, dividers, etc.

## Step 6: Optimize and Finalize

Before printing, review your layout for common issues.

### Checklist

- [ ] Measurements double-checked
- [ ] All items fit in planned bins
- [ ] Frequently used items near front
- [ ] Bin heights work with drawer depth
- [ ] Largest bins fit on print bed
- [ ] Color categories assigned (optional)
- [ ] Print list generated

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Bins too small**: Err larger. It's easier to have extra space than to cram items in.

**Ignoring drawer height**: A 6u bin in a 40mm drawer won't close.

**Over-optimizing**: Leave some flexible storage space. Not everything needs a dedicated bin.

**No access gaps**: Edge-to-edge bins are hard to remove. Leave a small gap at the front.

**Printing before measuring**: Always verify baseplate fit before printing all bins.
