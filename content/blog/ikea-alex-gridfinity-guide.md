# IKEA Alex Drawer Gridfinity Guide: Complete Setup for 2026

The IKEA Alex is one of the most popular desk drawer units in the world—and one of the most common targets for Gridfinity organization. This guide covers everything you need to transform your Alex drawers with 3D-printed modular storage.

<!-- IMAGE: IKEA Alex unit with one drawer open showing Gridfinity bins. Alt: "IKEA Alex drawer unit with Gridfinity bins installed" -->

## Quick Specs

| Alex Model | Drawer Internal Size | Grid Size | Height |
|------------|---------------------|-----------|--------|
| Alex (current) | 297 × 525mm | 7 × 12 | 58mm |
| Alex (older) | ~290 × 510mm | 6 × 12 | 55mm |

**Important**: IKEA has produced several Alex variants over the years. Measure your specific drawers before printing—dimensions can vary by 5-10mm between production runs.

## Why IKEA Alex + Gridfinity Works

The Alex drawer unit is popular for desk setups, but its drawers are essentially empty boxes. Without organization, items slide around every time you open the drawer.

Gridfinity solves this with:

- **Locked-in bins** that don't shift when the drawer moves
- **Customizable compartments** for your specific items
- **Modular flexibility** to reorganize without reprinting
- **Maximized space** compared to store-bought organizers

The 7×12 grid gives you 84 grid squares to work with—enough for serious organization.

## Step 1: Measure Your Specific Alex Drawers

Don't assume your Alex matches the "official" dimensions. IKEA's manufacturing tolerances mean your drawers might be a few millimeters different.

### What to Measure

<!-- IMAGE: Top-down photo of empty Alex drawer with measurement overlay. Alt: "IKEA Alex drawer measurement points" -->

**Internal width**: Measure at multiple points (front, middle, back). The narrowest measurement is your usable width.

**Internal depth**: Front to back. Check for the small lip at the front of some models.

**Internal height**: Bottom of drawer to the top rail. This limits your maximum bin height.

### Expected Measurements

**Current Alex model (2020+)**:
- Width: 295-300mm (usable: ~297mm)
- Depth: 523-528mm (usable: ~525mm)
- Height: 55-60mm

**Older Alex model (pre-2020)**:
- Width: 288-295mm
- Depth: 508-520mm
- Height: 52-58mm

If your measurements differ significantly, you may have a regional variant or different Alex product (Alex Nine, etc.).

### Converting to Grid

Standard Alex (297 × 525mm):
- Width: 297 ÷ 42 = **7.07 units** → use 7 units
- Depth: 525 ÷ 42 = **12.5 units** → use 12 units

This leaves:
- 3mm horizontal gap (7 × 42 = 294mm)
- 21mm vertical gap (12 × 42 = 504mm)

The gaps at the back of the drawer are fine—baseplates don't need to fill every millimeter.

## Step 2: Choose Your Baseplate Strategy

You have several options for covering the 7×12 grid:

### Option A: Multiple Standard Baseplates

Use combinations of standard baseplates:
- 2× (5×5) + 2× (2×5) + 2× (5×1) + 2× (2×1)

**Pros**: Readily available STL files, easy to print
**Cons**: Seams between plates, more print jobs

### Option B: Custom Full-Drawer Baseplate

Print the entire 7×12 baseplate in sections designed to tile perfectly.

Several makers have created Alex-specific baseplates:
- [Printables: Gridfinity Base for IKEA Alex](https://www.printables.com/model/213778)
- [MakerWorld: Slim Gridfinity Base for Alex](https://makerworld.com/en/models/28581)
- [MakerWorld: Alex Drawers Gridfinity Set](https://makerworld.com/en/models/592121)

**Pros**: Cleaner fit, designed for Alex dimensions
**Cons**: Must print exactly as designed, larger prints

### Option C: Baseless Approach

Skip baseplates entirely—just print bins and arrange them in the drawer.

**Pros**: Less printing, simpler
**Cons**: Bins can shift, less secure, harder to reorganize

### Recommendation

For most users, **Option B** (Alex-specific baseplates) offers the best experience. The community has already done the math, and these fit better than generic baseplates.

## Step 3: Select Bin Heights

Alex drawers are relatively shallow. Here's what fits:

| Bin Height | Fits Alex? | Best For |
|------------|-----------|----------|
| 2u (14mm) | Yes | Flat items, SD cards, coins |
| 3u (21mm) | Yes | Pens, USB drives, small tools |
| 4u (28mm) | Yes | Most items, cables, office supplies |
| 5u (35mm) | Yes (tight) | Taller items, bottles |
| 6u (42mm) | No | Too tall for standard Alex |

**Safe choice**: 3u and 4u bins fit all Alex variants with clearance.

**Maximum**: 5u bins (35mm) fit in most Alex drawers but may rub on drawers with lower internal heights. Test one before batch printing.

## Step 6: Print and Install

### Print Settings

| Setting | Recommendation |
|---------|----------------|
| Material | PLA or PETG |
| Layer height | 0.2mm |
| Infill | 15-20% (bins), 20-25% (baseplates) |
| Walls | 2-3 perimeters |
| Supports | Not needed for standard bins |

### Filament Estimate

For a full Alex drawer (7×12 grid):
- Baseplates: ~250-300g
- 20-30 mixed bins: ~200-400g
- **Total**: 450-700g (about half a 1kg spool)

See our [Gridfinity Filament Cost Guide](/blog/gridfinity-filament-cost) for detailed estimates.

### Installation Tips

1. **Clean the drawer** before installing baseplates
2. **Secure baseplates** with adhesive putty, VHB tape, or CA glue (optional—most stay in place without adhesive)
3. **Test fit baseplates** before adding bins
4. **Install bins** starting from the back, working forward
5. **Leave access space** at the front for easy removal

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

### "My baseplates don't fit"

**Cause**: Measurement variance between Alex units

**Solutions**:
- Sand edges of baseplates slightly
- Use smaller baseplates with gaps
- Check for obstructions (rails, screws)

### "Bins are too tall"

**Cause**: Alex height varies; 5u bins may not fit all units

**Solutions**:
- Stick to 4u bins (28mm)
- Measure internal height before printing tall bins
- Use lite/low-profile bin versions

### "Baseplates slide around"

**Cause**: Smooth drawer bottom, no friction

**Solutions**:
- Add adhesive putty underneath
- Use VHB tape at corners
- Print spacers to fill edge gaps
- Some Alex-specific baseplates include bumper features

### "Not enough grid space"

**Cause**: 7×12 feels limiting for large collections

**Solutions**:
- Use multi-layer approach (see [Planning Guide](/blog/how-to-plan-gridfinity-drawer-layout))
- Organize across multiple Alex drawers
- Use smaller bins, dividers

## FAQ

### How many Gridfinity units fit in an IKEA Alex drawer?

Standard Alex drawers fit a **7 × 12 grid** (84 total units). Older models may fit 6 × 12 (72 units).

### Do I need baseplates for Alex drawers?

Baseplates are recommended—they lock bins in place when the drawer opens/closes. Without them, bins can shift.

### Which bins are too tall for Alex?

Most Alex drawers are 55-60mm tall. Use **4u bins (28mm) or shorter** to be safe. 5u bins (35mm) work in some units.

### Can I buy pre-made Gridfinity for Alex?

Yes—Etsy sellers offer Alex-specific Gridfinity sets. However, 3D printing is more cost-effective if you have a printer.

### How much does it cost to Gridfinity an Alex drawer?

About **$10-15 in filament** per drawer (half a spool). Pre-made sets cost $50-100+.
